# Network Topologies

A **network topology** refers to the arrangement or layout of devices (nodes), links, and connections in a computer network. It defines how devices communicate and share data with each other, either physically (physical topology) or logically (logical topology).

The main types of network topologies are: **Bus, Star, Ring, Mesh,** and **Hybrid**.

---

## 1. Bus Topology

### Structure

- All devices (nodes) are connected to a single central cable, called the **backbone** or **bus**.
- Data sent by any device travels along the bus in both directions until it reaches the intended recipient.
- Both ends of the cable are terminated using a **terminator** to prevent signal reflection.

![[Pasted image 20260802161602.png]]

### Advantages

- Simple and easy to install.
- Requires less cable compared to other topologies, making it cost-effective.
- Suitable for small networks.

### Disadvantages

- If the main cable (backbone) fails, the entire network goes down.
- Performance degrades as more devices are added (increased traffic/collisions).
- Difficult to troubleshoot and identify faults.
- Limited cable length and number of devices.

---

## 2. Star Topology

### Structure

- All devices are connected individually to a central device such as a **hub, switch, or router**.
- Data from one device to another passes through the central device.

### Advantages

- Easy to install and manage.
- Failure of one node/cable does not affect the rest of the network.
- Easy to detect faults and remove devices without disrupting the network.
- Good performance for moderate-sized networks.

### Disadvantages

- If the central device (hub/switch) fails, the entire network goes down.
- Requires more cable compared to bus topology.
- Cost increases due to the need for a central device.

---

## 3. Ring Topology

### Structure

- Devices are connected in a circular manner, forming a closed loop.
- Each device is connected to exactly two other devices, forming a single path for data (unidirectional or bidirectional).
- Data travels around the ring from device to device until it reaches its destination.

### Advantages

- Data transmission is fast and orderly (no collisions in unidirectional rings using token passing).
- Easier to install and reconfigure than mesh.
- Equal access to the network for all devices (with token passing).

### Disadvantages

- Failure of one device or cable can disrupt the entire network (in a single ring without redundancy).
- Difficult to troubleshoot.
- Adding or removing devices can disrupt network operation.
- Data must pass through multiple nodes, causing delay in large networks.

---

## 4. Mesh Topology

### Structure

- Every device is connected to every other device in the network, either fully (full mesh) or partially (partial mesh).
- Provides multiple paths for data to travel between devices.

### Advantages

- Highly reliable — failure of one link/device does not affect the whole network.
- Provides high redundancy and fault tolerance.
- Data transmission is fast and secure due to dedicated links.
- Easy to identify faults in a specific link.

### Disadvantages

- Very expensive due to the large amount of cabling and hardware (I/O ports) required.
- Complex to install and configure.
- Difficult to manage and maintain as the network grows.
- Not practical for large-scale networks (full mesh) due to high resource requirements.

---

## 5. Hybrid Topology

![[Pasted image 20260802161646.png]]

### Structure

- A combination of two or more different topologies (e.g., Star-Bus, Star-Ring) to meet specific network requirements.
- Designed to leverage the advantages of the combined topologies while minimizing their disadvantages.

### Advantages

- Flexible and scalable — can be designed according to organizational needs.
- Reliable, as fault in one part of the network does not necessarily affect other parts.
- Combines the strengths of multiple topologies.
- Suitable for large organizations with diverse networking needs.

### Disadvantages

- Complex design and implementation.
- Expensive due to the need for additional hardware and cabling.
- Requires skilled personnel to manage and maintain.
- Troubleshooting can be difficult due to the complexity of the hybrid structure.

---

## Quick Comparison Table

|Topology|Structure|Fault Tolerance|Cost|Scalability|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Bus|Single backbone cable|Low|Low|Low|
|Star|Central hub/switch|Medium|Medium|Medium|
|Ring|Circular connection|Low–Medium|Medium|Low|
|Mesh|Fully/partially interconnected|High|High|Low (full mesh)|
|Hybrid|Combination of topologies|High|High|High|

---

## Summary

- **Bus** – simple, cheap, but a single point of failure.
- **Star** – reliable and easy to manage, but depends on the central device.
- **Ring** – orderly data flow, but a break can affect the whole network.
- **Mesh** – highly reliable and redundant, but costly and complex.
- **Hybrid** – flexible and combines benefits of multiple topologies, but complex and expensive.
