# 📶 Wireless Networking — Notes

> 📌 **Note:** Wireless networking lets devices communicate **without physical cables**, using radio waves to transmit data through the air.

---

## 🧠 What is Wireless Networking?

Wireless networks transmit data using **electromagnetic (radio) waves** instead of copper or fiber cables. This includes everything from home Wi-Fi to cellular networks to Bluetooth earbuds.

---

## 📡 Types of Wireless Networks

|Type|Range|Example Use|
|---|---|---|
|**WPAN** (Personal Area Network)|~10 m|Bluetooth, NFC|
|**WLAN** (Local Area Network)|~50–100 m|Wi-Fi (home/office)|
|**WMAN** (Metropolitan Area Network)|City-wide|WiMAX|
|**WWAN** (Wide Area Network)|Regional/global|4G, 5G cellular|

> 💡 **Tip:** The bigger the range, the **lower the speed** and the **higher the power consumption**, generally speaking.

---

## 📶 Wi-Fi (802.11 Standards)

Wi-Fi is the most common WLAN technology, standardized under **IEEE 802.11**.

|Standard|Marketing Name|Max Speed|Frequency|
|---|---|---|---|
|802.11n|Wi-Fi 4|~600 Mbps|2.4 GHz / 5 GHz|
|802.11ac|Wi-Fi 5|~3.5 Gbps|5 GHz|
|802.11ax|Wi-Fi 6 / 6E|~9.6 Gbps|2.4/5/6 GHz|
|802.11be|Wi-Fi 7|~46 Gbps (theoretical)|2.4/5/6 GHz|

### Frequency bands

|Band|Range|Speed|Interference|
|---|---|---|---|
|**2.4 GHz**|Longer range, better wall penetration|Slower|More crowded (microwaves, Bluetooth)|
|**5 GHz**|Shorter range|Faster|Less crowded|
|**6 GHz** (Wi-Fi 6E/7)|Shortest range|Fastest|Least crowded|

> ⚠️ **Trade-off:** Higher frequency = **faster speed** but **shorter range** and **worse wall penetration**.

---

## 🔐 Wireless Security Standards

|Standard|Security Level|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|**WEP**|❌ Broken|Old, easily crackable — **never use**|
|**WPA**|⚠️ Weak|Improved over WEP, but outdated|
|**WPA2**|✅ Good|Uses **AES encryption**, widely used today|
|**WPA3**|✅✅ Best|Current standard — stronger encryption, protects against brute-force attacks|

> ⚠️ **If your router still uses WEP or WPA, upgrade immediately** — these are considered insecure by modern standards.

### Key security terms

- **SSID** — the network's name (e.g. "Home_WiFi_5G").
- **Pre-Shared Key (PSK)** — the password used to join a WPA2/WPA3 network.
- **MAC Filtering** — restricts network access to specific device hardware addresses (easy to spoof, so not a strong security measure alone).
- **Hidden SSID** — hides the network name from casual scans (security through obscurity — not a real protection).

## 📱 Cellular Networks

|Generation|Approx. Speed|Key Feature|
|---|---|---|
|**2G**|Kbps|Basic calls/SMS|
|**3G**|~Mbps|Mobile internet begins|
|**4G / LTE**|~100 Mbps|Fast mobile broadband, streaming|
|**5G**|~1–10 Gbps|Ultra-low latency, IoT-scale connectivity|

> 💡 **5G isn't just "faster 4G"** — its key advantage is **massive device density support** and **very low latency**, which matters for things like autonomous vehicles and real-time IoT.

---

## 🔵 Bluetooth

- Short-range wireless protocol (~10 m typical, up to ~100 m for Bluetooth 5.0+ in open space).
- Operates in the **2.4 GHz** band.
- Used for peripherals: headphones, keyboards, mice, fitness trackers.
- **BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy)** — a power-efficient variant used in wearables and IoT devices.

---

## 📡 Other Wireless Technologies

|Technology|Purpose|
|---|---|
|**NFC (Near Field Communication)**|Extremely short range (~4 cm) — contactless payments, tap-to-pair|
|**Zigbee / Z-Wave**|Low-power mesh networking for smart home devices|
|**Satellite Internet**|Long-range internet via orbiting satellites (e.g. Starlink)|
|**Infrared (IR)**|Line-of-sight communication, mostly legacy (TV remotes)|

---

## 🧱 How a Wireless Connection Works (simplified)

```
1. Access Point (router) broadcasts its SSID
2. Device scans for available networks
3. Device sends connection request + authentication (PSK/password)
4. Router verifies credentials
5. Device is assigned an IP address (via DHCP)
6. Data now flows wirelessly between device ↔ router ↔ internet
```

## ⚠️ Common Wireless Issues

- **Interference** — from microwaves, other routers, thick walls, or too many devices on 2.4 GHz.
- **Channel congestion** — too many nearby networks using the same Wi-Fi channel.
- **Signal attenuation** — signal strength weakens with distance and obstacles.
- **Rogue Access Points** — unauthorized Wi-Fi networks set up to intercept traffic (a security risk).

> 💡 **Tip:** Changing your router's **channel** (especially on 2.4 GHz) can significantly reduce interference in crowded areas like apartments.

---

## 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Wi-Fi** = WLAN standard under IEEE 802.11; newer standards (Wi-Fi 6/6E/7) offer more speed and less congestion.
- **2.4 GHz = range, 5 GHz/6 GHz = speed** — that's the core trade-off to remember.
- **Always use WPA2 or WPA3** — never WEP or open (unsecured) networks for sensitive activity.
- **Cellular (4G/5G)** and **Wi-Fi** solve different problems: wide-area mobility vs. local high-speed access.
- **Bluetooth/NFC/Zigbee** are short-range technologies built for low-power, close-proximity communication rather than general internet access.
